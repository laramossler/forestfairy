# Observation Log Workflow

When Lara returns from a walk or wants to log observations, follow this process:

## Step 1: Debrief
Ask gentle questions to capture the experience:
- Where did you walk today? (Which part of the 50 acres?)
- What was the weather like? Temperature, wind, sky, precipitation?
- What caught your attention first?
- What did you hear? See? Smell? Feel?
- Any species to note? (Plants, birds, mammals, insects, fungi)
- Any changes since your last visit to this area?
- How did it feel to be there?

## Step 2: Write the Log Entry
Create or append to: `observations/2026/MM/YYYY-MM-DD.md`

Use this template:

```markdown
# Field Observation — [Date]

**Time**: [start] - [end]
**Weather**: [conditions, temp, wind, sky]
**Route/Area**: [where on the property]
**Moon Phase**: [if notable]

## Observations

### Flora
- [What plants were notable, any changes, blooming, dormancy]

### Fauna
- [Birds heard/seen, mammals, insects, tracks, scat, signs]

### Water
- [Creek level, flow, ice, puddles, drainage patterns]

### Soil & Ground
- [Moisture, frost, erosion, fungal activity, leaf litter]

### Microclimate Notes
- [Sun/shade patterns, temperature differences, wind exposure, snow retention]

## Species Log
| Species | Type | Location | Behavior/Notes |
|---------|------|----------|----------------|
| | | | |

## Reflections
[Personal notes, feelings, questions, connections to design work]

## Follow-ups
- [ ] [Things to investigate, research, or revisit]
```

## Step 3: Cross-Reference
After logging:
- Flag any new species → offer to add to species database
- Note any patterns connecting to previous observations
- Surface connections to PDC/practicum design work
- Suggest what to look for on the next visit to this area

## Step 4: Pattern Tracking
Over time, maintain awareness of:
- **Phenology**: When species leaf out, bloom, fruit, go dormant
- **Wildlife corridors**: Repeated animal movement patterns
- **Moisture mapping**: Where water collects, drains, persists
- **Sun/shade mapping**: How light changes across seasons
- **Disturbance patterns**: Storm damage, erosion, browsing pressure
- **Succession signals**: Pioneer species appearing, canopy gaps filling
