# Observation Log Skill Guide

## When to Use
- Lara comes back from a walk and wants to debrief
- She mentions something she noticed on the property
- She wants to review past observations or find patterns
- She asks for a weekly/monthly/seasonal summary
- She's connecting field observations to food forest design decisions

## Log Entry Structure
Each observation gets logged to `observations/YYYY/MM/YYYY-MM-DD.md`

```markdown
# Observation Log — [Date]

## Conditions
- **Time:** [time of observation]
- **Weather:** [temp, sky, wind, precipitation, recent weather patterns]
- **Moon phase:** [if notable]
- **Season notes:** [days since solstice/equinox, phenological markers]

## Location(s) on Property
[Describe where on the 50 acres — develop consistent naming over time: e.g., "north ridge", "creek bottom", "south-facing slope", "house clearing", "old logging road", "east meadow edge"]

## Observations

### Plants
| Species | Layer | Location | Status | Notes |
|---------|-------|----------|--------|-------|
[What's leafing out, flowering, fruiting, seeding, dormant, dying back]

### Birds
| Species | Location | Behavior | Notes |
|---------|----------|----------|-------|
[What was seen/heard, activity, numbers]

### Other Wildlife
[Mammals, insects, amphibians, fungi, tracks, scat, signs]

### Water & Soil
[Creek levels, soil moisture, drainage patterns, frost/thaw, erosion]

### Light & Microclimate
[Sun angles, shade patterns, wind corridors, temperature variations, frost pockets]

### Forest Health
[Canopy condition, deadfall, new growth, disease/pest signs, succession indicators]

## Connections to Food Forest Design
[How do today's observations inform the practicum design? What niches are revealed? What's already thriving where? What ecological functions are present or missing?]

## Questions & Follow-ups
[What to investigate further, what to check next visit, research needed]

## Reflections
[Personal notes, feelings, seasonal awareness, gratitude]
```

## Running the Log

### After Each Walk
When Lara debriefs after a walk:
1. Create the log entry with the date
2. Ask guiding questions to fill in details she might not mention:
   - "What was the light doing?"
   - "Did you notice any water movement?"
   - "Anything different from last time you walked that area?"
   - "Any sounds that stood out?"
3. Write the entry in Hazel's voice — grounded but attentive to beauty
4. Flag any new species for the plant or bird inventory
5. Note design-relevant observations

### Quick Logs
Sometimes Lara will just mention something in passing ("saw a pileated woodpecker today"). Still log it — create a brief entry even if incomplete. Incomplete data over time is still valuable.

## Pattern Recognition
Periodically (or when asked), review the log for:
- **Phenological patterns**: what blooms/fruits/migrates when
- **Microclimate mapping**: where is warmest, coldest, wettest, driest
- **Wildlife corridors**: repeated sightings that suggest movement patterns
- **Succession indicators**: what's establishing, what's declining
- **Seasonal rhythms**: how the property changes through the year
- **Design insights**: aggregate observations into actionable food forest decisions

## Summaries
When asked, generate summaries at various scales:
- **Weekly**: Quick recap of observations, new species, notable changes
- **Monthly**: Patterns emerging, phenological markers, weather trends, design insights
- **Seasonal**: Major ecological shifts, species inventory updates, food forest planning connections
- **Annual**: Full property portrait, year-over-year changes, design progress

## Connecting to the Practicum
Every observation is potential design data. When logging, always consider:
- Which food forest layer does this relate to?
- What ecological function is being demonstrated?
- Does this observation confirm or challenge a design assumption?
- What plant species might thrive in the conditions observed?
- How do indigenous management practices relate to what we're seeing?

**The observation log should become one of the most valuable inputs for the practicum food forest design.**
