Tall Oregon Grape

Mahonia aquifolium

Berberidaceae

Etymology

Named for Bernard M'Mahon, Irish-American horticulturist who helped popularize Lewis & Clark specimens. 'Aquifolium' means holly-leaved, for the spiny evergreen foliage.

Also known as: Oregon Grape, Holly-leaved Barberry, Mountain Grape
Native range: Pacific Northwest, British Columbia to Northern California

Form

Type: Evergreen shrub
Height: 3-6 ft
Spread: 3-5 ft
Growth: Moderate
Lifespan: 30+ years
Roots: Shallow, spreading rhizomes that colonize

Growing Conditions

Sun: Part shade to full sun (more fruit in sun)
Water: Moderate — drought tolerant once established
Soil: Adaptable — well-drained, tolerates poor soil
Zones: 5-9
Tolerance: Drought, shade, deer resistant
Pests: Generally pest-free. Possible rust, leaf spot in wet conditions.

Phenology

Leaf out: Evergreen
Bloom: March-April · bright yellow racemes · fragrant · pollinated by early bees
Fruit: July-September · blue-purple berries with waxy bloom · clusters
Dormancy: Evergreen

Human Uses

Food

Part: berries

Preparation: jelly, juice, wine, dried

Flavor: tart, grape-like

Harvest: July-September

Sustainable yield: 10-20 lbs/year

High in vitamin C. Best cooked — very tart raw.

Medicine

Part: root bark

Preparation: tincture, tea

Uses: berberine extract (antibacterial, digestive), liver support

Harvest: fall/winter

Sustainable yield: minimal - only from pruned/damaged plants

Powerful antimicrobial. Consult herbalist for dosage.

Craft

Part: inner bark and roots

Preparation: dye extraction

Product: yellow dye

Harvest: spring

Sustainable yield: from pruned branches only

Cultural & Traditional Use

Used by Coast Salish and Plateau peoples for food and medicine. Berries eaten fresh or dried, root bark for skin conditions and digestive issues.

Propagation

  • rhizome division (90% success)
  • semi-hardwood cuttings Aug-Sep (50-70%)
  • seed (20-40%, slow)

Where to Source

Raintree Nursery, One Green World, local native plant sales, easy to propagate from existing specimens

On the 50 Acres

Thriving in understory throughout the 50 acres. Particularly dense stands along edges and in partial sun. Excellent candidate for guild expansion with comfrey and native strawberry groundcover. Already supporting robust bird and pollinator populations. Could sustainably harvest 15-20 lbs berries annually without impacting wildlife.
